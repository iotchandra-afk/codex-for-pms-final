# Restore a fair chance to respond

Product brief · Rook Dispatch · Proposed owner: incoming Dispatch PM  
Decision requested from Helen: sponsor a bounded readiness-and-recovery pilot for design and engineering validation.

## Problem and evidence

A missed offer can be scored as refusal, lower future priority and remove the opportunities needed to recover. Farlight, Vesper, The Undertow and Meteor Mite fall from 49 combined offers/week before release week to three in the final week. Overall acceptance recovers to 72.7%, concealing that loss of access. Module 4 reproduced the mechanism in 16 tests; historical readiness, eligibility and actual ranking state remain unobserved.

Kip needs to understand why Meteor Mite's phone is quiet and what can be done. Mite needs an actionable route back without being promised work that belongs to a better-qualified response.

## Proposed experience

**Dispatch Ready Review** connects explanation, readiness confirmation and an operational decision. Kip sees verified offer history and the Gale/Mite contrast, requests a review, and follows its status. Mite confirms readiness for that review. Operations checks availability, capability, region, travel and Supply maintenance, then authorizes one appropriate offer within a two-hour review window. Every action has a visible receipt. Missing or changed fit blocks or pauses the path.

The two-hour window and one-offer limit are prototype policy proposals, not approved production rules. A matched opportunity is never guaranteed. The prototype uses an illustrative incident and makes no live routing change.

## Policy and scope

Preserve taken, explicitly declined and missed as distinct outcomes. In the proposed recovery experience, a miss triggers follow-up rather than silently becoming refusal or unavailability. Historical outcomes stay intact. Score recovery must be an explicit, auditable policy; readiness confirmation alone does not reset history.

Scope: one handler, Meteor Mite, one operational review and one matched opportunity. A controlled 75-second example compares the old 90-second and current 60-second windows. The demo recovery offer uses a proposed 90-second window; engineering must validate its effect on incident escalation.

Exclude fleet-wide resets, guaranteed allocations, unrelated Supply workflow redesign and production deployment.

## Success and rollout gates

Primary outcome: eligible, available responders regain appropriate offer exposure. Pair it with time since last eligible offer, missed-versus-declined mix, callout take rate and assignment latency; headline acceptance alone cannot declare recovery. Establish numeric pilot targets and abort thresholds with Ravi before launch; the current extract cannot measure eligibility-adjusted exposure or answer time.

Helen approves policy; Marcus/Wen verify implementation, hard capability gating and rollout; Nadia/Sofia validate the customer journey. The Supply owner must verify the shared availability record's shape and meaning. Preserve readiness separately from history and test the actual consumer contract before release.

**Deliverable:** [working prototype](prototype.html), with historical evidence, simulated review/offer states, an audit receipt and blocked/expired paths. Prototype completion demonstrates the experience; it does not establish pilot effectiveness.
