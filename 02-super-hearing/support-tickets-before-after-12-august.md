# Support tickets before and after 12 August

Compared all 147 tickets by filing date, using the existing 56-issue primary classification. Before: 29 June–11 August, 40 tickets over 44 calendar days. Release day: 12 August, 2 tickets, kept separate. After: 13 August–7 September, 105 tickets over 26 calendar days. Counts are tickets, including follow-ups, not unique customers or incidents.

## What changed

- Filing rate increased from 0.91 to 4.04 tickets per calendar day, about 4.4 times. This compares the full available windows; it is not a statistical causal estimate.
- Low/no offer reports: 0 before, 30 after (13 no offers for days; 17 fewer offers than usual). Four responders appear: Farlight, The Undertow, Corporal Ashgrove and Halfmoon. First filed on 17 August.
- Offers disappearing before an answer: 0 in this specific primary class before, 1 on release day, 14 after, spanning 10 responders after the cutoff. A separate July signal-loss ticket (#3014) already described a missed ping; #3043 also says similar events happened earlier in the year. Symptoms cannot be assumed to have begun with 4.2.
- The two offer-related primary classes account for 44/105 (41.9%) of after-period tickets. Two expiry tickets (#3110, #3140) also describe quiet stretches; they are counted once, under expiry.
- Filters shift from two before-period persistence requests to 11 after-period usability/reliability reports: resets 4, another handler's filters 2, hidden active indicator 2, clearing difficulty 2, cross-computer behavior 1. A further cross-computer ticket is on release day. Two after-period entries thank Rook for persistence.
- Duplicate phone notifications: 3 before, 0 after. This is consistent with the 4.2 release note about a duplicate-notification fix, but absence of later tickets does not prove resolution.
- Existing UI and Supply concerns remain: dark mode 2 before/2 after; small badges, screen readers, identical console alert sounds, catalog search, failure-report feedback and urgent vest replacement each have 1 before/1 after.
- Repeated quiet-stretch complaints extend into September, with reports of normal availability and tags. Filing dates and customer-reported counts do not establish the underlying routing mechanism or population-wide prevalence.

## Executed prompt findings

The comparison uses filing dates: before = 29 June–11 August (44 calendar days); after = 13 August–7 September (26 days); the two 12 August tickets remain separate.

The ticket rate rises about 4.4 times, while named handlers/responders rise only from 11 to 12. These are support participants, not counts of the entire customer base. Follow-ups and multiple issues per customer contribute to the volume.

Across the 56 primary classifications, 18 first appear strictly after 12 August, 2 first appear on release day, 26 appear in both comparison windows, and 10 have no tickets after the cutoff. First appearance in this dataset does not establish when a problem began.

### Changes with dated evidence

1. **Missing/reduced offers:** 0 before; 30 after, including 13 reporting silence and 17 fewer offers. Four handlers report on four responders: Farlight 10 tickets, The Undertow 10, Corporal Ashgrove 5 and Halfmoon 5. These are repeated reports rather than 30 independently affected customers. Quote: “Farlight has had four pings in the past seven days. Before the middle of August she was getting about twelve a week.” — #3071, 20 August.
2. **Offers disappearing before an answer:** 0 before in this specific classification; 1 on release day; 14 after, naming 10 responders. Quote: “I would ask that whoever reviews this at least confirm the timing is what it is meant to be, since it felt very fast.” — #3043, 13 August. That same ticket says this happened earlier in the year, and #3014, 14 July, describes a ping lost while out of signal.
3. **Filters:** 2 before-period requests to retain filters; after-period problems split into resets 4, wrong handler's settings 2, weak active-filter indicator 2, no clear-all control 2 and cross-computer behavior 1. A further cross-computer report was filed on release day. Quote: “Twice now my saved filters have been wiped overnight.” — #3044, 13 August. Two after-period tickets appreciate persistence: “Having the console keep my filters has saved me a few minutes every shift.” — #3100, 26 August.
4. **Earlier duplicate-notification reports stop appearing:** 3 before, 0 after. Baseline quote: “The Longcast's phone went off a second time for a job he had already answered.” — #3007, 6 July. This matches the direction expected from the release note's fix; it is not proof the issue is eliminated.
5. **Existing concerns continue:** dark mode has 2 tickets before/2 after; status-badge readability, screen-reader names, identical alert sounds, failure-report feedback and urgent protective-equipment prioritization each have 1 before/1 after. Equal counts over unequal windows do not establish unchanged rates. Quote: “How do I mark something as genuinely urgent?” — #3074, 21 August.
6. **Additional issues first recorded after the cutoff:** availability time zones, phone-number validation, photo updates, slow coverage loading, password resets, additional handler access, history exports, busy-day maintenance, late maintenance reminders and week-long approval waits each have 0 before/2 after. A generic browser-tab title has 0 before/1 after. These require investigation, not automatic attribution to 4.2. Quote: “The reminder that Ironvale's harness was due for servicing arrived two days after the service date.” — #3057, 16 August.

### Language, urgency and repeated reports

The offer-related subset shifts toward account-confidence questions and requests for an explanation despite correct availability and tags. “nothing again this week. starting to wonder if im still even in the system.” — #3120, 1 September. “Nothing looks wrong on our side. Could someone check yours?” — #3131, 3 September.

A quiet stretch followed by a lost offer appears in #3110 (29 August) and #3140 (5 September). “Given how long she had been waiting, this landed badly.” — #3110. Both tickets have expiry as their primary classification; both also support the quiet-stretch theme. The two primary offer-related classes total 44/105 after-period tickets (41.9%) without double counting.

This is not a claim that all earlier tickets were minor: urgent damaged-equipment requests already existed. It is a qualitative change in the offer-related reports, not a measured sentiment score.

### Three consequential changes

1. A new concentration of reports that Dispatch is not offering responders work, persisting into September.
2. Reports spanning 10 responders that live offers disappear before an answer, including two reports compounding long quiet stretches.
3. A substantially higher support workload, with new saved-filter failure modes alongside the intended improvement.

The strongest supported conclusion is a post-release change in reported problems and support demand. Causal diagnosis requires ping/offer data, responder cohorts and routing verification; tickets alone cannot distinguish seasonality, routing changes and shorter wait times.
